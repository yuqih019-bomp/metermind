# Developer-product launch research

## Cases reviewed

The launch patterns were compared across nine public examples from indie makers including Marc Lou, Tibo, Danny Postma, Pieter Levels, Rob Hallam, Jack Friks, and Axel Schapmann, plus developer-tool launch retrospectives on Reddit and DEV Community.

## Repeatable patterns

1. **Lead with a result or tension.** Strong launches start with a concrete outcome, a surprising before/after, or a painful question—not a generic announcement.
2. **Show the product.** A real screenshot, short demo, or GIF makes the promise credible and immediately understandable.
3. **Sell one outcome.** The best posts focus on one job: save launch time, make logos, find a cost leak, or prioritize feedback.
4. **Use proof honestly.** Real usage, real examples, and clearly labeled sample data outperform unsupported claims. Never fake revenue or savings.
5. **Reduce risk.** Privacy, no-account setup, transparent pricing, and a clear one-time purchase remove objections.
6. **Give one next step.** One CTA per post. On X, keep the main post link-free and put the product link in the first reply.
7. **Start a conversation.** Ask a specific problem question and answer every useful reply; early conversations matter more than passive likes.
8. **Adapt to the platform.** X rewards concise visual hooks; Reddit expects a useful story and honest discussion; Product Hunt needs a clear tagline, gallery, and personal maker comment.
9. **Launch is a sequence.** Follow the announcement with problem insights, demos, user objections, and product iterations instead of repeating the same sales pitch.
